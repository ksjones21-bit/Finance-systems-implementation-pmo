## 1. Project Background

Horizon Community Health invested in a cloud-based financial planning and reporting platform to improve departmental budget visibility, reporting efficiency, and forecasting capabilities.

Nine months into implementation, the organization continues to rely on manual Excel reporting. Inconsistent account mappings, unclear process ownership, reconciliation discrepancies, and limited internal system knowledge have prevented the organization from realizing the expected benefits.

The current monthly reporting process requires approximately five business days, including two days dedicated to investigating discrepancies.

A phased 60-day recovery initiative is proposed to improve reporting reliability, establish governance, and strengthen internal ownership of the reporting process.

## 2. Project Objectives

The project will pursue the following objectives:

1. Establish clear accountability for reporting-system governance.
2. Improve the accuracy and consistency of financial reporting.
3. Reduce the monthly reporting cycle from five to three business days.
4. Develop internal capability to maintain approved account mappings.
5. Demonstrate reliable departmental reporting through a controlled pilot.
6. Establish repeatable reconciliation, testing, and change-management procedures.

## 3. Project Scope

### In Scope

- Current-state reporting process assessment
- Risk-based account mapping review
- Prioritized mapping corrections
- General ledger-to-planning-system reconciliation
- Reporting cutoff and data refresh procedures
- Mapping governance and change-control procedures
- Technical impact assessment and user acceptance testing
- One departmental budget-to-actual reporting pilot
- Staff training and knowledge transfer
- Operational handoff to the Finance Director

### Out of Scope

- New reporting automation development
- Forecasting models and forecasting-tool enhancements
- Replacement of accounting, payroll, or planning platforms
- Enterprise-wide system redesign
- Comprehensive historical data cleanup
- Advanced analytics and new dashboard development

Requests outside the approved scope will be documented for future consideration and evaluated through the change-control process.

## 4. Project Governance

| Role | Accountability |
|---|---|
| CFO | Executive sponsorship, resource authorization, escalation decisions |
| Finance Director | Business ownership, priorities, material change approval, operational acceptance |
| Senior Financial Analyst | Mapping preparation, reconciliation, testing, process documentation |
| Accounting Manager | Account classification validation and financial accuracy approval |
| IT Director / Systems Analyst | Technical impact assessment, integration testing, technical release clearance |
| Implementation Consultant | Configuration assistance, training, technical documentation |
| Business Transformation Project Lead | Project coordination, milestone tracking, risk management, stakeholder reporting |

The Finance Director retains accountability for ongoing operations and may delegate routine activities to designated team members.

## 5. Resource Assumptions

- Senior Financial Analyst: approximately 10 hours per week, subject to budget-cycle constraints.
- Implementation Consultant: approximately 20 remaining contracted hours.
- Accounting and IT: participation in scheduled reviews and testing, with capacity to be confirmed.
- Finance Director: availability for prioritization, approvals, and escalation.
- Additional consultant spending requires separate authorization.

Resource assumptions must be validated before the baseline project schedule is approved.

## 6. Implementation Roadmap

| Phase | Timeline | Key Activities | Deliverables |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1: Validate & Prioritize | Days 1–15 | Confirm baseline, review mappings, establish governance, prioritize discrepancies | Approved governance approach; prioritized issue register |
| Phase 2: Pilot & Demonstrate | Days 16–30 | Correct priority mappings, train analyst, reconcile pilot report, conduct testing | Validated departmental report; draft operating procedures |
| Executive Checkpoint | Day 30 | Review pilot results, resources, risks, and remaining scope | Decision to proceed, adjust, or request resources |
| Phase 3: Expand & Stabilize | Days 31–60 | Extend approved processes, validate reporting, finalize documentation, transition ownership | Operational procedures; expanded reporting capability; final assessment |

Progression beyond Day 30 requires executive review of pilot results and remaining constraints.

## 7. Success Measures

| KPI | Baseline | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly reporting cycle | 5 business days | 3 business days |
| Material unexplained reconciliation differences | Baseline to be confirmed | Zero at sign-off |
| Mapping governance | Informal | Approved responsibilities and procedures |
| Departmental reporting pilot | Not validated | One accepted report by Day 30 |
| Internal maintenance capability | Consultant-dependent | Trained, authorized internal personnel |
| Operational ownership | Unclear | Formal acceptance by Finance Director |

Targets will be assessed against available reporting-cycle evidence. Achievement is not guaranteed by the proposed schedule.

## 8. Change Management and Release Controls

Proposed mapping changes will follow a controlled process:

1. Analyst documents the proposed change and expected impact.
2. Accounting validates the financial classification.
3. IT evaluates technical dependencies where applicable.
4. Changes are tested in a nonproduction environment where available.
5. Finance reconciles affected reporting outputs.
6. Accounting signs off on financial accuracy.
7. Finance Director authorizes material reporting changes.
8. Authorized personnel deploy changes and verify production results.
9. Change documentation, approvals, and rollback instructions are retained.

Historical reporting impacts will be evaluated before previously distributed reports are revised.

## 9. Key Project Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Inaccurate financial reports | Source reconciliation, testing, documented sign-off |
| Unintended downstream system impacts | IT impact review, dependency testing, controlled release |
| Limited analyst availability | Capacity planning, weekly monitoring, escalation |
| Insufficient consultant hours | Prioritize knowledge transfer and essential configuration work |
| Scope expansion | Approved scope boundaries and formal change requests |
| Delayed approvals | Defined decision authority and escalation procedures |

Risk owners, likelihood, impact, and response triggers will be maintained in the project RAID register.

## 10. Communication and Decision Framework

**Weekly project working session:** Review progress, issues, risks, and upcoming decisions.

**Weekly sponsor update:** Summarize milestone status, resource constraints, significant risks, and requested decisions.

**Day 30 executive checkpoint:** Evaluate pilot acceptance, resource consumption, and readiness for expansion.

**Day 60 closeout review:** Assess results, unresolved issues, operational readiness, and follow-on recommendations.

## 11. Project Acceptance and Operational Handoff

The project will be eligible for closure when:

- Required governance procedures have been approved.
- Agreed reconciliation and reporting acceptance criteria have been evaluated.
- Priority changes have been tested and documented.
- Internal personnel have demonstrated required maintenance activities.
- Outstanding risks and issues have assigned operational owners.
- The Finance Director formally accepts responsibility for ongoing reporting operations.
- The CFO receives a final performance assessment against project objectives.

Unmet targets or unresolved material issues must be disclosed in the closeout assessment and assigned a documented disposition.

Approval of this charter authorizes the project team to proceed within the agreed scope, schedule, and resource constraints.
